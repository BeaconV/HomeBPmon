Blood pressure monitors for home use

Monitors approved by the British and Irish Hypertension Society (BIHS), with links to Amazon UK

If you check your blood pressure at home, use a monitor you can trust. Monitors approved by the BIHS have been tested and shown to meet international accuracy standards. The BIHS says the NHS and NICE recommend these monitors for home use. (BIHS: Find a blood pressure monitor you can trust)

This page lists all 227 monitors on the BIHS home-use list. Use the search box and filters to narrow it down. (BIHS approved monitor list, updated 1 November 2026)

Before you buy

  • Check the exact model number. Many monitors look alike or have similar names. Compare the model number on the box or listing with the one in this list.
  • An upper-arm monitor is usually the best choice. Most monitors on the list go on your upper arm. If you're not sure which type to get, ask your GP practice or pharmacist.
  • Get the right cuff size. Measure around the middle of your upper arm and check the cuff fits. A cuff that's too small or too big can give the wrong reading.
  • Replace an old monitor. The BIHS advises replacing a home monitor after 4 years at most, or sooner if it's damaged (especially the cuff) or you're worried it's not accurate. (BIHS: Monitor life)

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I take a reading at home?

Follow the instructions that came with your monitor. Take a reading and write it down, wait 1 minute, then take another. You usually do this twice a day, once in the morning and once in the evening. Your doctor or nurse will tell you how many days to do this for. (NHS: Checking your blood pressure at home)

What do my readings mean?

A reading has 2 numbers, for example 130/80. When you check it at home, a reading of 135/85 or higher is usually considered high. Share your readings with your GP practice so they can look at the whole picture. (NHS: What blood pressure readings mean)

My monitor says it can detect an irregular heartbeat. Is that approved too?

Not always. For several monitors, the BIHS notes that it has only approved the blood pressure reading. Extra features, like atrial fibrillation (AF) or irregular heartbeat detection, haven't been checked for accuracy. If your monitor warns you about an irregular heartbeat, speak to your GP practice. (BIHS approved monitor list)

I'm pregnant. Can I use any monitor on this list?

Not necessarily. The BIHS has a separate list of monitors approved for pregnancy and other specialist uses. Ask your midwife or GP practice which monitor to use.

When to ask for help

  • Contact your GP practice or a pharmacy for a non-urgent check if your home readings are often high, or you think you might have high blood pressure. (NHS: High blood pressure)
  • Get help from NHS 111 if you often have headaches or blurred vision, have chest pain that comes and goes, or have other symptoms you're worried could be caused by high blood pressure. (NHS: When to get help)
  • Call 999 if you have sudden chest pain that doesn't go away, pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw, stomach or back, or chest pain with sweating, feeling sick, light-headed or short of breath. (NHS: Urgent symptoms)
